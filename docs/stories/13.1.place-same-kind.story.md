### The mechanism (chosen): the draw reads the word before the place and draws only names that fit it

1. **Read the word** (`core/place_word.py`, new). For each place, the word right before it, or at the head of its span, is one of the epic's words, « à », « en », « de », « d' », « du » (for « du » and « au »); one of Q4's words, « le », « la », « l' », « les » (for « les », « des » and « aux ») and « région »; or no such word. Capitals are ignored (« À », « En », « Du », « La Région »); « d’ » and « l’ » (typographic apostrophe) count as « d' » and « l' »; a lone capital « A » counts as « à » (L6); a lowercase « a » is the verb and counts as no such word.
2. **Which occurrence sets the word.** The draw happens once per place and document, so one word is chosen per place, in document order (in a spreadsheet, the cells' order, then the position in the cell; in any other document, the position):
   - the epic's word of the place's first occurrence; if that occurrence has none, the epic's word of the first later occurrence that has one;
   - if no occurrence has an epic word, Q4's word of the first occurrence that has one;
   - otherwise no such word.
   - This stays within the epic's rule. Under the epic, Q4's words count as "no such word", and "no such word" asks only for a name without an article, which every name this story draws is. So a place first seen in « TechSolutions France » and later in « en France » gets a name that reads right in both places (« TechSolutions Normandie », « en Normandie ») instead of a city that breaks the second (« en Lyon »). Likewise a place first seen after « La Région » and later after « en » is drawn for « en » (a feminine region, which also reads right after « région »), not for « région », whose names include masculine ones (« en Périgord »).
   - In the dev corpus this moves the mentions that fit their word from 258 to 263 of 263 (Measurements). Ranking Q4's words with the epic's instead gives the same 263, since no corpus place has a Q4 word before an epic word; the order above is the SM's call, for the reason just given.
3. **Which names fit each word** (`pseudonym/place_names.py`, new; the French lists L1 to L5, approved by Lionel). Every list is read at draw time from the loaded library, in the library's own order.
4. **Draw in tiers.** Each word has an ordered list of tiers (L3). The draw tries the first tier (up to 100 random draws, each passing `accepts_value`, as today), then the next. Within a word, names whose first letter is of the same class as the real place's (vowel or consonant) come first: a place called « Angers » gets a name starting with a vowel even after « à », so a later « d'Angers » still reads right.
5. **No free name.** Once every tier of an epic word or of no such word is used up (or refused by Story 11.6), the place gets `Location-NNN`, as today. No name that reads wrong after the word is ever drawn for it. "Another fitting class" before `Location-NNN` is built into the tiers: « de » falls back from cities to feminine regions, « d' » likewise, « à » from the same-letter cities to the other cities, « en » from the same-letter regions to the others. « du » or « au » has one tier only (`neutral`: 19 names, the 4 masculine regions and Q5's 15).
   - **Q4's words** (Lionel's (b)): their preferred tiers come first, then the no-word row's tiers (without the names already tried), so they reach `Location-NNN` only where no such word would, never sooner. Once their preferred names are used up, a city can be drawn after them (« pour le Lyon »), as today.
6. **The check knows the word.** `accepts_value(value, "LOCATION", place_word=...)` refuses a library place that does not fit the word. A value from no library list (a fallback name, a custom value) is judged as today. Story 11.5's review proposals pass the place's word, so the review list keeps a pseudonym only if a draw would give it for that word, and "the list shows what is written" still holds.
7. **Every path passes the word** (Fix design 4): CLI process and batch, desktop single and batch, spreadsheets, the review's previews, the desktop's review refresh, the untaken redraw, Story 11.5's proposal check. Story 11.7's replace flow has no document: it draws as for no such word, with the stored real place's first letter. The review's previews and the desktop refresh read the words from every place the end will receive, not from one row per name (the desktop single-document review shows one row per name, and the end receives every place of it).
8. **`star_wars` and `lotr`.** The same rules on their names, with each list given a role (L4): a city-like list serves « à », a region-like list « en », « du » and « au », both serve « de » (consonant) and « d' » (vowel). Their invented names have no French gender, so every region-like name serves both « en » and « du »/« au ». Q4's words take region-like names (Lionel's (b): "themes: their region-like names"; L3b).

**`french_geography.json` is not used, not even as a fallback** (the SM's call, which the epic leaves open). The word, not the kind of place, decides which names fit; the geography file knows only 187 of the corpus's 263 place mentions, and Lionel found the kind-based list "useless".

**Why this one:**

| Changing the word itself (« à Le Mans » → « au Mans ») | yes | yes | **changed**: the preposition is usually outside the span; the review list shows the pseudonym only; DOCX, PDF and spreadsheet cells are written from the span, so the review list and the written file would disagree (Story 11.5) | untouched | rejected |

- Cities do not serve « en »: « en Avignon » and « en Arles » exist in regional usage, but « en Lyon » is wrong, and the rule cannot tell them apart.
- Masculine and plural regions do not serve « de » or « d' » (« du Grand Est », « des Hauts-de-France »). Plural regions serve no such word, « les », « des », « aux » and « région ».
- "Then the no-word row": its tiers follow, without the names already tried, so a Q4 word never reaches `Location-NNN` before no such word would.
- English: after each word, only the names in its row are drawn, in that order.

**L4. `star_wars` and `lotr`: which list plays which role, and invented names.**
- `star_wars`: `cities` are city-like; `planets` and `regions` are region-like. `lotr`: `cities` and `planets` (the sites: « Weathertop », « Helm's Deep », « Moria »…) are city-like; `regions` (the realms: « Rohan », « Gondor »…) are region-like.

Region-like names serve « en », « du » and « au »: the names are invented, so they have no French gender, and the story takes « en Naboo », « au Kamino », « au Gondor » and « du Rohan » as reading right. - English: a theme city or site follows « à »; a theme planet, region or realm follows « en », « du » and « au ».

**L5. - English: the letter class sets « de » or « d' », as French elision does.

### Known limitations (shown to Lionel with the examples)

- **The first word decides** (the epic's Known limit). A place keeps the pseudonym chosen for the word of its first occurrence (mechanism 2), in that document and in every later one. In the dev corpus, 17 of the 160 places of a document stand after two different words (16 plus « Normandie », whose later « La Région » is now a word); with this story, 263 of 263 mentions fit their word inside their document. Read as one database across the corpus (documents in name order), 254 of 263 do: the 9 misses are « en France » ×4, « en Allemagne » ×2 and « en Europe » ×2, where the place got a city in an earlier document that had no word before it (« TechSolutions France »), and « depuis les US », where « US » got a city in an earlier document.
  - **The cost of "an epic word beats a Q4 word"** (mechanism 2; PO, v2.3). It helps « La Région Normandie … en Normandie » (a feminine region, right in both) but hurts a place seen first after « la », « l' » or « région » and later after « de » or « d' », which is drawn for « de » or « d' »: « la Gironde … de Gironde » gives « la Metz … de Metz », « l'Ardèche … d'Ardèche » gives « l'Angers … d'Angers », « La Région Normandie … de Normandie » gives « La Région Metz … de Metz ». Ranking the first word of either kind instead gives « la Normandie … de Normandie » and « l'Alsace … d'Alsace » (right in both), « La Région Bretagne … de Bretagne » in 9 of 30 draws (« … de Périgord » in the other 21), but « La Région Périgord … en Périgord » in 21 of 30 draws. The dev corpus has no place with a Q4 word before an epic word (0 of 160), so both rankings give 263 of 263 there.
- **Words the epic does not list** (« le », « la », « l' », « les », « des », « aux », « région »): Q4 (b). Once their preferred names are used up, they draw from the no-word row, so a city can follow them (« pour le Lyon »), as today. « le » shares its 19 names with « du » and « au » (and « région » draws them too, R1), and « les » has 2.
- **Small pools.** In `neutral`, the 20th distinct place after « du » or « au » in one database gets `Location-001` (« au Location-001 »); after « d' », the 14th; after « en », the 15th. Places after « le » use the same 19 names, and places after « région » can (R1), so they bring that point closer. Story 11.6's rule can make it sooner. The numbers are in Pool sizes. The 25 dev documents read as one database use 4 of the 19 (« du Havre », « du Sud », « du 7ème », « pour le Nord »).
- **Spreadsheets in the desktop app, and DOCX tables.** These are read as one text, with a tab between cells and a line break between rows (`utils/file_handler.py`), and the word reader looks back over both. A place at the start of a cell can then take the last word of the cell before it, when that word is one the reader knows (a cell « en » before a cell « France »). The place then gets a name of that word, which also reads right alone in its own cell, since no drawn name has an article. The command line reads a spreadsheet cell by cell and is not affected.
- **The word the detector leaves.** The word is read in the text as written. If detection cuts « Le Havre » to « Havre » after « du », the word is « du » and the pseudonym a masculine region (« du Grand Est »), which reads right. If it keeps an article inside the span (« La Réunion »), the article is replaced with the name.
- **A first occurrence the user removes.** The review draws for the word of the first occurrence detected; the end draws for the first occurrence sent. When the user rejects or retypes the first occurrence and the next one has another word, the end refuses the shown pseudonym if it does not fit, draws again, and reports the change (Story 11.5's report).
- **A command-line [T] retype** keeps showing the old type's pseudonym, as today (Story 11.5's T10, `tests/unit/test_review_list_written_pseudonym.py:680-694`): the end draws the place for its word and reports the change. The desktop list updates at once (P11). A place added by hand or a text edited in the command-line review is drawn for its own word (Fix design 4, T-cli-added).
- **Themes:** the English words in some invented names (« Outer Rim Territories », « Misty Mountains ») stay.
- **Not changed:** `mapping_key` still strips only the straight « d' » (`utils/french_patterns.py:27`); detection is out of scope. The word reader reads both apostrophes.

**2. New module `gdpr_pseudonymizer/core/place_word.py`** (LF; no logging).
- `word_before(text: str, start: int, end: int) -> str`: if `text[start:end]` starts (after spaces) with « d' », « d’ », « l' » or « l’ », or with « au », « aux », « du », « des », « de », « à », « en » or a lone « A » each followed by a space (as `FRENCH_PREPOSITION_PATTERN` requires `\s+` after them; PO, v2.3), that word. Mapping, capitals ignored except « A »: « à » and a lone capital « A » → `A`; « en » → `EN`; « de » → `DE`; « d' » → `D`; « du », « au » → `DU`; « le » → `LE`; « la » → `LA`; « l' » → `L`; « les », « des », « aux » → `LES`; « région » → `REGION`; anything else (« a », « et », « pour », a company name, punctuation, the start of the text) → `NO_WORD`.
